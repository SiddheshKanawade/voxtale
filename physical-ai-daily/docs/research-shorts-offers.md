## Mapping to our Shorts (INTERPRETATION: our reasoning, not Hormozi's or YouTube's words)

### M1. The "offer" in a promo Short, through the value equation
The offer is "tap through and watch this week's episode". Each term, from the viewer's side:

| Term | What it means for a P2A viewer | How a Short raises or lowers it | Grounding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dream outcome** | Understand the one surprising robot/AI thing this week and why it matters, from footage and papers, without hype. Be the person who actually knows | Name the specific payoff the episode adds that the Short doesn't: the how and why, the caveats, the numbers. Not "lots of robotics news" | A1.1, A1.6 |
| **Perceived likelihood** (proof) | "This channel will actually deliver that" | (a) The Short itself fully pays off its own hook, since free content is judged as a sample of the paid thing; (b) on-screen receipts: real footage, paper/source screenshot with the claim highlighted, named lab; (c) no clickbait mismatch | A1.7, A2.10, A3.1 |
| **Time delay** | How long until the episode gives them what the Short promised | Link straight to the episode. The episode's story segment opens on the Short's promise. Ideally the cold open or chapter 1 covers that story; otherwise say "it's the first story" and use a chapter timestamp | B12 (5–10 s rule), A1.3 |
| **Effort & sacrifice** | Taps, hunting, committing 10+ minutes | Use the **Related video** link (one tap, clickable, under the handle), never "link in description" (B11). One CTA only. Say how long the segment is ("3-minute breakdown") to shrink perceived effort | B10, B11, A2.12 |

### M2. Short structure = Hook → Retain → Reward → one CTA
- **Hook (0 to ~2 s): topic + headline.** Our topics are always *recent* or *trending* (A2.5). Each hook should use at least two of the seven components (A2.7). Natural fits: **Recency** (this week) + **Unusual** (a robot doing something odd) / **Celebrity** (Tesla, Figure, DeepMind, a named researcher) / **Conflict** (claim vs reality, lab vs lab) / **Ongoing** (a race still running). Value-equation hooks also work (A1.6): outcome + number + time, e.g. "learned X in 2 hours of video". Measure it with Stayed-to-watch % (B3/B4). That is Hormozi's own hook metric (A2.3) under YouTube's name.
- **Retain: one embedded open question.** Use a 3-item list, steps, or the story of the demo (A2.9). Measure with APV and AVD (B1, B4). Keep our 5–7 s visual-change cap.
- **Reward: answer the Short's own question completely** (A2.10). Give the *what*; the episode sells the *how, why, and what it means* (A3.1, A3.2). Never hold the payoff hostage behind the link. It breaks the reward, and it lowers perceived likelihood for the next CTA.
- **CTA (last ~3 s, once): clear, exact action, reason now** (A3.3; B12's wording). Template: "The full breakdown (how it works and what's still fake) is in this week's episode. Tap the video link above." Point at or flash the episode thumbnail (B12). Only this one ask (A2.12): no like/subscribe/comment stack.

### M3. Length
Make it as long as the story needs, up to 3:00 (B7, B9). Value per second decides, not a target length (A2.10). For promos, we expect 30–60 s to suit one story better. That's our assumption, untested; check it with youtube-stats.

### M4. Give/ask balance
Hormozi argues for giving more and asking less (A2.11). YouTube argues for a CTA in every Short that links long-form (B12). Our resolution: every Short gets a complete reward plus exactly one soft CTA at the end. Hormozi himself ran heavy promotion on top of normal content during his launch (A3.6). Watch subscriber and Related-video click data for fatigue.

### M5. Operating rules derived from the official guidance
- Set the Related video on every promo Short (needs advanced features: verify the channel has them).
- Post Shorts close to the episode release. Recency helps in the Shorts feed (B14) and for news (B8).
- Don't delete and re-upload underperformers (B8). Make a better version instead.
- Don't invest in Shorts thumbnails beyond a good frame (B8, B17).
- Judge each Short by: stayed-to-watch %, APV/AVD, Related-video clicks, and the episode's "Shorts → long-form" traffic in analytics.
